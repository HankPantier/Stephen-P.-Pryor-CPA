## Start your business with a CPA who sets the foundation right

Starting a business in Port Chester means a hundred decisions before you've made a dollar: what entity to form, how to track expenses, whether your books will hold up under IRS scrutiny. Get any of it wrong early and you're paying to fix it later, usually at a worse time than now.

Stephen P. Pryor, CPA built Business Foundation Services around a simple idea: you run your business, we'll run your numbers, starting from day one instead of day two hundred. New owners in Westchester County and beyond come to us before they've filed paperwork, not after they've made a mess of it. That's the point.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-right | image: entity-structure-consultation.jpg | alt: "CPA reviewing business entity paperwork with a new small business owner" | query: "accountant consulting new business owner documents" -->
## Choose the right entity structure from day one

LLC, S-corp, partnership, sole proprietorship: each one changes how you're taxed, what you owe in self-employment tax, and how much paperwork you'll file every year. Pick wrong and restructuring later means legal fees, new filings, and sometimes a tax bill you didn't see coming.

Stephen and the team walk new owners through [entity type analysis](/what-we-do/entity-type-analysis) before anything is filed, weighing state requirements against federal tax exposure so the structure fits the business you're actually building, not a generic template. For a contractor forming an LLC or a restaurant owner deciding between S-corp and partnership status, this is the conversation that saves five figures down the road.

<!-- block: content-split | variant: image-left | image: accounting-system-setup.jpg | alt: "Small business owner setting up bookkeeping software with an accountant" | query: "small business owner accounting software setup" -->
## Set up accounting systems built for how you work

Most accounting messes don't happen because an owner is careless. They happen because the system never matched how the business actually operates: invoices sent from three different apps, receipts stuffed in a shoebox, payroll tracked in a spreadsheet nobody trusts.

During onboarding, we learn your internal workflows first: how you invoice, how you pay vendors, how cash moves through the business week to week. Then [accounting system setup](/what-we-do/accounting-system-setup) gets built around that reality, not against it. The result is a system you'll still be using in year three, feeding clean numbers into [bookkeeping](/what-we-do/bookkeeping) and tax prep without a rebuild every time something changes.

## Built for construction, real estate, and hospitality startups

A general accounting setup treats every new business the same. New owners in construction, real estate, and hospitality need something more specific, and that's where most of our foundation work happens.

### Construction
icon: Hammer

New contractors need job costing built in from the first invoice, not retrofitted after three jobs run over budget with no visibility into which one made money. We set up systems that track cost per project alongside payroll and cash flow, so profitability by job is visible from month one. See our full [construction accounting](/industries/construction) approach.

### Real estate
icon: Home

Entity choice and depreciation planning matter enormously for real estate owners, and getting them wrong at formation limits your options later, including cost segregation and 1031 exchange strategy. We structure new real estate entities with those future moves in mind. More on how we support [real estate accounting](/industries/real-estate) clients.

### Bars and restaurants
icon: Wallet

New bar and restaurant owners need accounting systems that talk to their POS from day one, with tip reporting and sales tax handling built in rather than bolted on after the first audit notice. We've set this up for enough bars and pubs to know where it breaks. Details on [restaurant accounting](/industries/restaurants).
