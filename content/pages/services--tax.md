## Tax planning and preparation built around your life or business

Most tax prep shops show up in March and disappear in April. That works fine if your only tax question is "how much do I owe," but it falls apart the moment you're running a construction company with three jobs in progress, a rental portfolio, or a bar with tip reporting to track. Stephen P. Pryor, CPA, LLC handles tax planning and preparation as a year-round relationship, not a once-a-year transaction.

Every client works from a secure online platform where you can see your financial data in real time, not just at filing season. Business and individual tax services are bundled into one fixed monthly fee, so there's no surprise invoice when a question comes up in July or a tax notice lands in October. You get a CPA who already knows your numbers, because we've been looking at them all year.

The goal is the same across every business engagement: fewer surprises, more planning, and a return that reflects decisions made months in advance — not scrambled together at the deadline.

## Business tax services for construction, real estate, and hospitality companies

Business tax preparation only works if the person preparing it understands how your business actually makes money. A general contractor's tax return should reflect job costing and profitability by project, not just a lump total of revenue and expense. A real estate investor's return needs to account for depreciation schedules that actually match the properties held. A bar or restaurant's return needs to reflect thin margins and cash flow that moves differently than a typical retail business.

Stephen P. Pryor, CPA, LLC prepares corporate and business tax returns with that context built in from the start, drawing on construction, real estate, and hospitality clients across Westchester County and the surrounding region. Planning happens throughout the year, not just before the filing deadline, so entity-level decisions, timing of major purchases, and depreciation strategy get addressed while there's still time to act on them.

This is business tax preparation and planning, delivered with the industry context to back it up. It's paired with [bookkeeping](/services/bookkeeping) and [payroll](/services/payroll) support, so the numbers feeding your return are accurate before they ever reach a tax form.

## Entity type analysis for new and growing businesses

Whether a new business should be an LLC, S-corp, or something else isn't a question with one right answer. It depends on projected income, how many owners are involved, whether real estate is held inside or outside the entity, and how the business plans to grow. Get it wrong early and it can mean years of paying more tax than necessary or restructuring later at a real cost.

Entity type analysis at Stephen P. Pryor, CPA, LLC walks new business owners through that decision with state and federal tax implications spelled out in plain terms, not jargon. It's especially relevant for construction companies weighing liability exposure, real estate investors deciding whether to hold property in separate entities, and restaurant or bar owners setting up a new location.

This service pairs naturally with [business foundation services](/what-we-do/business-foundation-services) for anyone launching a company from scratch, and with ongoing tax planning once the entity is in place. Learn more about [entity type analysis](/what-we-do/entity-type-analysis).

## Tax strategy for construction, real estate, and bar and restaurant owners

### Construction
icon: Hammer

Job costing complexity makes it hard to know which projects are actually profitable until the return is already filed. Tax planning for contractors ties into project-level tracking throughout the year, so income and expense timing, equipment purchases, and payroll costs are accounted for before year-end, not discovered after. Visit our [construction industry page](/industries/construction) for more.

### Real Estate
icon: Building2

Depreciation is one of the biggest tax levers a real estate investor has, and it's also one of the most commonly missed. Cost segregation opportunities and 1031 exchange timing both require planning well ahead of a transaction, not a scramble after closing. See how we support [real estate investors and owners](/industries/real-estate).

### Bars and Restaurants
icon: Receipt

Thin margins leave no room for guesswork on sales tax or tip reporting compliance. Bar and restaurant tax preparation has to reflect cash handling realities and multi-jurisdiction sales tax rules, tracked consistently so nothing gets flagged at filing time. Learn more on our [restaurant and bar industry page](/industries/restaurants).
