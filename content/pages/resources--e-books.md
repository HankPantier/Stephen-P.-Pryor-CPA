## Practical resources built from real client questions

Every guide in this library started as a real question, asked at the kitchen table of a new restaurant, in a job-site trailer, or over a spreadsheet full of rental property numbers. Stephen Pryor, Shawn Wilson, and Denise Romano write these ebooks the same way they'd answer a phone call: plain language, real numbers, no jargon for the sake of sounding smart. Download what fits your situation today. Then call when you're ready to talk specifics, because a guide can explain a concept, but only a CPA who knows your books can tell you what it means for your bottom line.

## Guides for construction and real estate businesses

Contractors and real estate investors deal with a different set of numbers than most small businesses: job costing, retainage, depreciation schedules, and cash that doesn't show up until a project phase closes. These guides break down the parts that trip people up most. The firm's [construction](/industries/construction) and [real estate](/industries/real-estate) teams can walk through your specific numbers anytime.

### The New KPIs of Business
photo: contractor-financial-dashboard.jpg
query: contractor reviewing financial dashboard site

Revenue alone doesn't tell you whether a job is actually profitable. This guide covers the metrics that matter for tracking job costs and margins across multiple projects running at once.
[Read more](/resources/e-books/the-new-KPIs-of-business)

### The Small Business Owner's Guide to Inflation
photo: construction-materials-cost-increase.jpg
query: construction site material costs rising

Material costs, subcontractor rates, and financing terms have all moved fast in recent years. This guide covers where to adjust pricing, reserves, and project bids so a phase-to-phase cash crunch doesn't catch you off guard.
[Read more](/resources/e-books/the-small-business-owner-guide-to-inflation)

## Resources for bar, pub, and restaurant owners

Bars and restaurants run on thin margins and thick paperwork: tip reporting, sales tax on food versus alcohol, and a cash drawer that needs to reconcile every night. The firm built its [restaurant](/industries/restaurants) practice around exactly these headaches, with a specialty in bars and pubs.

### Everything You Need to Know About 1099s
photo: bartender-tip-reporting.jpg
query: bartender counting cash tips

Most bars and restaurants run a mix of tipped employees, part-time staff, and contract workers, and getting the classification wrong gets expensive fast. This guide breaks down who gets a W-2, who gets a 1099, and what the IRS expects from each.
[Read more](/resources/e-books/everything-you-need-to-know-about-1099s)

### Protecting Your Financial Data: A Small Business Owner's Guide
photo: restaurant-pos-system-security.jpg
query: restaurant point of sale system

Between POS systems, payroll platforms, and online ordering, a bar or restaurant has more financial data moving through more places than most businesses realize. This guide covers the basics of locking it down before a breach becomes a bigger problem than a slow Tuesday night.
[Read more](/resources/e-books/protecting-your-financial-data-a-small-business-owner-s-guide)

## Bookkeeping, payroll, and entity formation guides for small business owners

Getting the foundation right before you're three years in with a mess to untangle saves real money later. These guides cover bookkeeping basics, payroll compliance, and how to think about entity structure from day one.

### How to Read a Financial Statement
photo: small-business-owner-financial-statement.jpg
query: small business owner reviewing financial statement

A balance sheet and a profit and loss statement tell two different stories about your business. This guide explains what each number actually means, so decisions come from your numbers instead of a guess.
[Read more](/resources/e-books/how-to-read-a-financial-statement)

### Cybersecurity on a Shoestring Budget
photo: small-business-data-security-laptop.jpg

Payroll files, bank logins, and client records are worth protecting even without an IT department on staff. This guide covers practical, affordable steps for locking down your accounting system as the business grows.
[Read more](/resources/e-books/cybersecurity-on-a-shoestring-budget)

The firm's [bookkeeping](/what-we-do/bookkeeping), [payroll](/what-we-do/payroll), and [business foundation](/what-we-do/business-foundation-services) services pick up right where these guides leave off, including [entity structure guidance](/what-we-do/entity-type-analysis) and [accounting system setup](/what-we-do/accounting-system-setup).

## Tax planning guides for individuals and business owners

Tax planning works best as a year-round conversation, not a scramble every April. These two guides cover both sides of it: personal deductions and business strategy.

### Maximizing Your Tax Refund: A Strategic Guide to Tax Planning
photo: individual-tax-planning-documents.jpg
query: person reviewing tax documents home

Most missed deductions come from decisions made in July, not April. This guide walks through planning moves that actually affect your refund, from retirement contributions to timing income and expenses.
[Read more](/resources/e-books/maximizing-your-tax-refund-a-strategic-guide-to-tax-planning)

### Making the Move to Subscription-Based Pricing
photo: business-owner-pricing-strategy-meeting.jpg
query: business owner reviewing pricing strategy

Switching a service-based business to recurring revenue changes how income gets recognized and taxed. This guide covers what to plan for before making the shift, so the tax bill doesn't surprise you the following spring.
[Read more](/resources/e-books/making-the-move-to-subscription-based-pricing)

The firm's [tax](/what-we-do/tax) and [personal income tax](/what-we-do/personal-income-tax) teams build projections around these strategies year-round, including [retirement plan analysis](/what-we-do/retirement-plan-analysis) for business owners planning ahead.

## Frequently Asked Questions About eBooks | Stephen P. Pryor, CPA

**Q: Are Pryor CPA's ebooks free to download?**
A: Yes. Every ebook in this library, covering bookkeeping, payroll, construction accounting, real estate tax strategy, and restaurant bookkeeping, is free. They're written by Stephen Pryor, Shawn Wilson, and Denise Romano based on the questions clients bring to the firm's Port Chester office.

**Q: Do you have guides specific to construction and real estate businesses?**
A: Yes. Guides like The New KPIs of Business and The Small Business Owner's Guide to Inflation address job costing, cash flow between project phases, and rising material costs. For 1031 exchange and depreciation questions specifically, the real estate advisory team works through the numbers one-on-one.

**Q: What resources are available for bar and restaurant owners?**
A: The library includes a guide on 1099 classification for tipped and contract staff, plus a data security guide for POS and payroll systems, both common pain points for bars and restaurants managing thin margins and sales tax complexity across food and alcohol sales.

**Q: Can I get a recommendation for which guide fits my business?**
A: Yes. Contact the Port Chester office and describe what you're working through, whether it's payroll for a seasonal restaurant staff or entity structure for a new construction company, and the team will point you to the right guide or set up a consultation.
