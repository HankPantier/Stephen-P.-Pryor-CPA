## AI made receipt fraud easier to fake and harder to spot

A recent report found that 40% of workers admit to using AI tools to create fake expense receipts. Five years ago, that number would have seemed impossible. Today, generative AI can produce a convincing restaurant receipt, gas station printout, or vendor invoice in seconds, complete with realistic totals, tax lines, and logos.

For business owners who approve reimbursements with a quick glance, that visual check is losing its power. A receipt that looks legitimate no longer means it is. This isn't a large-corporation problem. Any business that reimburses employees, contractors, or vendors for out-of-pocket costs is exposed, and most small and mid-sized companies still rely on manual review as their only safeguard.

## Why small and mid-sized businesses in Westchester County are especially exposed

Construction, real estate, and hospitality businesses process a high volume of reimbursable expenses: job materials, subcontractor payments, per diems, vendor deposits. That volume, paired with the fast pace of running a jobsite or a bar, often means expense review happens quickly and without much structure.

A construction company juggling several active projects may not have time to verify every material receipt against a purchase order. A bar or restaurant owner handling cash tips, vendor deliveries, and staff reimbursements at the same time has even less bandwidth. These are exactly the conditions where a fabricated receipt slips through: high transaction counts, thin administrative staffing, and trust built on years of working with the same vendors and crew.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: with-image | image: hands-sorting-expense-receipts.jpg | alt: "Close-up of hands sorting through a stack of paper expense receipts" | query: "hands sorting expense receipts paperwork" -->
## Warning signs your expense reporting process may be vulnerable

Most fake receipts don't announce themselves. They show up as small inconsistencies that get missed when review happens fast. A few patterns worth a second look:

- Receipts from the same vendor with inconsistent formatting, fonts, or logo placement
- Suspiciously round dollar totals with no itemization
- Missing tax lines, order numbers, or other details a real receipt would include
- Expenses submitted weeks after the purchase date with no explanation
- The same employee submitting reimbursements just under an approval threshold, repeatedly

None of these confirm fraud on their own. But a pattern of them is a signal that your current review process, whatever it is, needs a closer set of eyes on it.

## Simple steps to strengthen your expense controls today

You don't need a full systems overhaul to close the gaps AI-generated receipts exploit. A few practical changes go a long way:

- Require a second approver for reimbursements over a set dollar amount
- Match receipts against corresponding bank or credit card charges before reimbursing
- Ask for itemized receipts rather than accepting a total-only printout
- Run a periodic spot-check on a sample of past reimbursements, not just new ones
- Set up your accounting system so expense categories and vendors are consistent, making outliers easier to spot

Each of these takes minutes to put in place and none of them requires new software or a new hire, just a bit more structure around a process that may currently run on trust alone.

**Q: Can AI really generate fake expense receipts?**
A: Yes. A recent report found 40% of workers admit to using AI tools to create realistic-looking expense receipts, complete with logos, tax lines, and vendor details. These fakes are increasingly hard to catch through casual visual review, which is why more businesses are adding structured bookkeeping oversight to expense approval.

**Q: Which types of businesses are most at risk of receipt fraud?**
A: Construction, real estate, and hospitality businesses face higher exposure because they process large volumes of reimbursable expenses, like job materials, vendor payments, and per diems, often with limited internal controls. High transaction volume combined with manual review makes fabricated receipts easier to slip through unnoticed.

**Q: What are red flags of a fake expense receipt?**
A: Watch for inconsistent formatting between receipts from the same vendor, suspiciously round totals, missing tax or itemization details, and receipts submitted well after the expense date. None of these alone confirms fraud, but a pattern of them warrants a closer look from your bookkeeping team.

**Q: How can a CPA firm help prevent expense fraud?**
A: A bookkeeping and advisory partner adds a layer of human review that AI-generated fakes struggle to pass, cross-checking receipts against vendor records, bank activity, and spending patterns. Real-time access to your books through a secure platform makes irregularities easier to catch before reimbursement goes out.
