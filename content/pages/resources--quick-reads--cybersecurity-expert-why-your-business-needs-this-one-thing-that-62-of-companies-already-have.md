## Your financial data is a bigger target than you think

A recent report found that 62% of companies already use some form of formal cybersecurity protection, which means the remaining 38% are sitting exposed with far more to lose than they realize. If you run a business in Port Chester, your books hold client Social Security numbers, bank account access, payroll files, and vendor payment details. That's a far richer target than a typical retail database.

Most small business owners assume hackers go after big corporations. In reality, smaller companies get targeted precisely because their defenses are thinner. A single compromised email attachment containing a payroll file can expose every employee's personal information in seconds. The risk isn't hypothetical. It's sitting in your inbox and your accounting software right now.

<!-- block: industry-cards | variant: 3-col | theme: ink -->
## Where construction, real estate, and hospitality businesses are most exposed

### Construction
icon: Hammer

Job-cost data and vendor payment files move between project managers, subcontractors, and accounting constantly. That back-and-forth creates openings for invoice fraud, where a criminal intercepts an email thread and redirects a payment to a fake account before anyone notices.

### Real Estate
icon: Home

Tenant applications, lease files, and closing documents carry Social Security numbers, bank routing details, and signed contracts. A single breached email account tied to a property management system can expose dozens of tenants' financial records at once.

### Bars and Restaurants
icon: Receipt

Point-of-sale systems process card data all night long, and tip and payroll records sit right next to them. Weak POS security or shared logins turn a busy Friday shift into a data exposure event nobody catches until the chargebacks start.

## What our secure platform means for your financial data

Email attachments and shared spreadsheets are two of the easiest ways financial data leaks out of a small business. A file sent to the wrong address, or a spreadsheet copied one too many times, can put payroll numbers or bank details in the wrong hands with no way to pull it back.

Our secure online platform replaces that guesswork with one encrypted place for your real-time financial data.

- Access controls that limit who on your team sees payroll or banking details

## Simple steps to strengthen your business's financial security

You don't need an IT department to close the biggest gaps. A handful of habits, done consistently, cover most of the risk that catches construction, real estate, and hospitality businesses off guard.

- Use unique, strong passwords for banking and payroll logins, not the same one across every account
- Limit who on your staff can access bank feeds, payroll files, or vendor payment systems
- Review your bank and credit card feeds weekly instead of waiting for month-end
- Vet any software vendor before connecting it to your accounting system
- Turn on two-factor authentication anywhere it's offered, especially for banking and email
