## A CPA who listens before he advises

Stephen Pryor has spent over three decades building a CPA practice around one simple idea: numbers only make sense once you understand the business behind them. Before he offers a single recommendation, Stephen asks questions. What keeps you up at night? Where does cash get tight? Which vendor payment always seems to land at the worst possible time?

That listening-first approach shapes everything at the firm. When a new client comes on board, Stephen and his team take time to learn how that business actually runs, its billing cycle, its payroll quirks, its busy season, before touching a single spreadsheet. Clients don't get generic advice pulled from a textbook. They get guidance built around how their business actually operates, from someone who took the time to ask first.

## Deep roots in Westchester County and beyond

Port Chester is home base, but the firm's reach extends well past Westchester County. Stephen works with business owners throughout the county, along with clients in Manhattan and Brooklyn who want a CPA who still picks up the phone. The firm also serves clients in Asbury Park, New Jersey, and individuals and business owners across New York, New Jersey, Connecticut, and Florida.

That geographic range isn't an accident. Referrals travel, and clients who relocate don't want to start over with a new accountant every time they move. Stephen's clients keep working with him whether they're managing a job site in Port Chester or running a bar two states away.

## Specialized experience in construction, real estate, and hospitality

Stephen built his practice around three industries where the accounting gets complicated fast: construction, real estate, and hospitality, with particular depth in bars and pubs.

### Construction
icon: Hammer

Job costing separates a profitable project from one that quietly loses money. Stephen tracks profitability phase by phase, works through payroll complexities tied to certified payroll and job-specific rates, and helps contractors spot cash flow gaps before they become a problem.

### Real Estate
icon: Home

Depreciation strategy and cost segregation can mean the difference between an average tax year and a great one. Stephen works with real estate owners and investors on 1031 exchange timing, entity structuring, and the kind of planning that protects gains instead of handing them to the IRS.

### Bars and Restaurants
icon: Receipt

Thin margins leave no room for guesswork. Stephen helps bar and restaurant owners get a clear read on where money actually goes, stay compliant on tip reporting and cash handling, and untangle sales tax rules that trip up even experienced operators.

## Community involvement and long-standing trust

Stephen has practiced as a CPA since 1990, long enough to have handled tax law changes, recessions, and a pandemic without losing sight of what clients actually need. That tenure shows up in the trust he's built across Westchester County, including a seat on the Greenwich First Advisory Boards, where local business owners and advisors compare notes on the issues facing the community.

Stephen's client list isn't limited to for-profit business owners. He's handled tax and accounting work for nonprofits and PTOs, detail-heavy work that doesn't make headlines but keeps community organizations running. That range, from bar owners to booster clubs, says something about how Stephen approaches the job: adapt to the client, not the other way around.
