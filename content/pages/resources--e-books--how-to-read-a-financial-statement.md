## Why understanding your financial statements matters

Financial statements aren't paperwork you file away after your CPA signs off. They're the clearest window into whether your business is actually making money. A general contractor juggling three job sites at once needs to know which project is profitable and which one is quietly bleeding cash. A landlord with a handful of units in [real estate](/industries/real-estate) needs to see if rising insurance costs are eating into margin before the next mortgage payment comes due. A [bar](/industries/restaurants) owner watching a packed Friday night needs to know if that crowd actually shows up as profit on the income statement come month-end.

Reading a balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow statement isn't an accounting exercise reserved for CPAs. It's how owners in [construction](/industries/construction), real estate, and hospitality make faster, better-informed calls about hiring, spending, and growth, without waiting on someone else to translate the numbers first.

**Q: What are the three main financial statements?**
A: The three core financial statements are the balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow statement. The balance sheet shows what a business owns and owes at a point in time, the income statement shows profit or loss over a period, and the cash flow statement tracks how cash actually moved in and out.
