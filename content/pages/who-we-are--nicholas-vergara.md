## Nicholas Vergara's role on our Port Chester CPA team

Nicholas Vergara is senior manager at Stephen P. Pryor, CPA, working directly with business owners across Port Chester and the rest of Westchester County. He oversees client accounts day to day, coordinating bookkeeping, payroll, and tax work so nothing gets missed between the parts. Clients often call Nicholas first, whether the question is about a payroll deadline, a vendor bill, or where the cash went last month.

The firm bundles bookkeeping, payroll, and advisory support into one fixed monthly fee, and that model only works if someone is tracking every piece for every client. That's Nicholas's job. He also manages client access to the firm's secure online platform, so business owners can check real-time numbers instead of waiting for a report at month's end.

For a contractor mid-project, a landlord managing several properties, or a bar owner closing out a Friday night, that kind of steady oversight is the difference between guessing and knowing.

## Industry focus in construction, real estate, and hospitality accounting

Nicholas built his client base around three industries the firm has served for years: construction, real estate, and hospitality, with particular depth in bars and pubs. He knows the numbers that matter in each one and the mistakes that cost owners money.

### Construction
icon: Hammer

Job costing is where construction bookkeeping usually breaks down. Nicholas tracks profitability by project and phase, so owners can see which jobs actually made money and which ones quietly ate the margin. He also works through the payroll complexity that comes with union rates, prevailing wage, and multiple job sites.

### Real Estate
icon: Home

Depreciation schedules, cost segregation opportunities, and 1031 exchange timing all affect how much of a real estate investor's income actually gets taxed. Nicholas reviews these details with clients before decisions get made, not after a return is already filed.

### Bars and Restaurants
icon: Receipt

Thin margins leave no room for guesswork. Nicholas helps bar and restaurant owners get visibility into where money is actually going, keep tip reporting and cash handling compliant, and stay ahead of sales tax filings that trip up so many hospitality businesses.

## A relationship-driven approach to client service

Nicholas spends real time during onboarding learning how a client's business actually runs: the systems they already use, the reports the owner actually looks at, the parts of the books that stress them out. That groundwork is what lets the relationship work year-round instead of resetting every tax season.

He listens for the problem behind the question. A client asking about a QuickBooks report is usually really asking whether they can afford to hire another employee. Nicholas treats it that way. Clients get a person who already knows their business, not a new voice every time they call, and that continuity is part of what the firm means when it says it removes the stress from the numbers.

## Experience with bookkeeping, payroll, and tax support

Nicholas handles the daily and weekly work that keeps a client's books accurate and their filings on time. His work spans:

- Daily transaction classification, expense tracking, and invoicing for [bookkeeping](/what-we-do/bookkeeping) clients across construction, real estate, and hospitality
- Complex [payroll](/what-we-do/payroll) processing and filings, including multi-site crews and tipped employees
- Business [tax](/what-we-do/tax) planning and preparation for small and mid-sized companies, coordinated year-round rather than filed once and forgotten
- Reconciliations and reporting that feed directly into the firm's secure online platform, so clients see accurate numbers without waiting on a monthly close

This is the practical, hands-on work behind the firm's advisory conversations. Nicholas makes sure the underlying numbers are right before anyone talks strategy.
