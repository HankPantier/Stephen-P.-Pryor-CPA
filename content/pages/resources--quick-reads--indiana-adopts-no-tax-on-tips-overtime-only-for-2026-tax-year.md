## What Indiana's new law changes for 2026

Indiana just passed a law exempting tip income and overtime pay from state income tax, effective with the 2026 tax year. It's a narrower move than the federal "no tax on tips" proposals that circulated in Washington: Indiana's exemption applies only at the state level, and only to those two specific categories of income. Servers, bartenders, and hourly staff who earn overtime in Indiana will see a difference in their state withholding once the law takes effect.

For most business owners outside Indiana, this reads like a small regional headline. It isn't. State legislatures watch each other closely, and tip and overtime taxation has become one of the more active areas of state tax policy over the past two years. If you run a bar, restaurant, or any business with tipped or hourly staff in New York, New Jersey, or Connecticut, this is worth five minutes of your attention.

## Why this matters even outside Indiana

State tax rules on tips and overtime don't move in isolation. When one state changes how it treats tipped wages, other states tend to face pressure, either from workforce competition or from legislators pointing to it as a model. New York, New Jersey, and Connecticut all have their own tip credit rules, overtime thresholds, and reporting requirements already, and none of them mirror each other exactly.

For a bar or restaurant owner in Port Chester, that patchwork is the real issue. You might employ staff who live in Connecticut, run payroll under New York wage and hour rules, and watch federal proposals shift the ground again. Each layer changes independently. Indiana's move is a signal that tip and overtime taxation is unsettled territory right now, not a one-time headline. Owners who track these changes as they happen have more room to adjust withholding, staffing costs, and pricing before a rule catches them off guard rather than after.

## Tip income and overtime reporting complexity for hospitality employers

Tip and overtime rules create more day-to-day payroll work than most owners expect, especially once a business operates in more than one state or has staff who split time between tipped and non-tipped duties. The parts that tend to trip owners up include:

- Calculating the correct tip credit against minimum wage under state-specific rules
- Reconciling reported tips against credit card and POS tip data for accuracy
- Tracking overtime for employees whose pay includes both a base wage and tips
- Filing payroll taxes correctly across multiple states or locations
- Keeping W-2 and 1099 reporting consistent with actual tip income received

We handle payroll processing and filings for bars, restaurants, and hospitality clients across Westchester County, and tip reporting compliance is one of the most common places we find errors during onboarding. A missed tip credit calculation or an inconsistent filing across two locations can turn into a bigger problem at tax time, which is exactly the kind of gap our [payroll services](/what-we-do/payroll) are built to close.

**Q: Does Indiana's no-tax-on-tips law affect businesses outside Indiana?**

**Q: When does Indiana's tip and overtime tax exemption take effect?**
A: The exemption applies starting with the 2026 tax year. It removes state income tax on qualifying tip income and overtime pay earned by employees working in Indiana, separate from any federal tax proposals on the same topic.

**Q: How does tip reporting affect payroll for bars and restaurants in Westchester County?**
