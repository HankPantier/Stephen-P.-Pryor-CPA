## Why your refund timeline may differ

Refunds rarely arrive on a fixed schedule. A handful of common triggers explain most delays or changes to the amount you expected:

- Amended returns, which the IRS processes on a separate, slower timeline than original filings
- Credits claimed, like the Earned Income Tax Credit or Child Tax Credit, which require additional verification
- Entity type, since S-corps, partnerships, and sole proprietorships each flow through differently at tax time
- Withholding errors from payroll, which can shrink or inflate a refund without an obvious explanation
- Offsets for past-due taxes, child support, or other government debts
- Identity verification holds, especially for first-time filers or accounts with prior fraud flags

None of these mean something went wrong. They mean the return needs a closer look, and that's exactly the kind of detail Shawn Wilson, CPA and Denise Romano, CPA review with clients every filing season.

## Refund support for construction, real estate, and hospitality businesses

Business refunds work differently than personal ones, and the details shift again depending on your industry.

### Construction Companies
icon: Hammer

Retainage, job costing, and multi-phase contracts all affect how estimated payments line up with what a construction company eventually gets back. A refund tied to overpaid quarterly estimates often signals it's time to revisit cash flow projections between phases, not just file and forget. Our [construction](/industries/construction) clients get help matching job-level profitability to what shows up on the return.

### Real Estate Investors
icon: Building2

Depreciation schedules, cost segregation studies, and 1031 exchanges can all shift a refund up or down in ways that surprise even experienced investors. If a like-kind exchange wasn't structured correctly, an expected refund can turn into an unexpected bill. We review [real estate](/industries/real-estate) filings line by line before assuming a refund number is final.

### Bars and Restaurants
icon: Receipt

Thin margins, tip reporting, and sales tax complexity make refund timing especially unpredictable for bars and restaurants. Payroll tax filings tied to tipped employees can also affect what a business is owed. Our [restaurants and pub](/industries/restaurants) clients get a straight answer on what their refund reflects and what it doesn't.
