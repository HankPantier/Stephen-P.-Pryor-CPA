## What changed with the foreign earned income exclusion

The IRS adjusts the foreign earned income exclusion (FEIE) for inflation nearly every year, and the recent bump matters if you're a U.S. citizen or resident working abroad. The exclusion rose from $126,500 to $130,000, meaning qualifying taxpayers can shield that much more foreign wage or self-employment income from federal tax. Pair that with the foreign housing exclusion, and someone renting an apartment in Lisbon or running a consulting gig out of Singapore has real room to lower taxable income before credits even enter the picture.

For Port Chester and Westchester County residents with a spouse working overseas, a rental property abroad, or a remote contract with an international client, this isn't abstract. It's the difference between a tax bill that reflects your full foreign salary and one that reflects only the portion above the exclusion. The mechanics haven't changed. The dollar amount has, and that's worth revisiting with your CPA before you file.

## Who qualifies for these foreign income tax breaks

Qualifying for the FEIE comes down to passing one of two IRS tests. Neither is complicated once you see how the IRS actually applies them.

- **Physical presence test**: You were physically present in a foreign country (or countries) for at least 330 full days during any 12-month period. Vacation days back in Westchester County count against you, so the math needs to be exact.
- **Bona fide residence test**: You established a genuine, uninterrupted residence in a foreign country for an entire tax year, even if you traveled back to the U.S. occasionally.

We see this most often with clients who split time between a business abroad and family in Port Chester, or small business owners managing international contracts while still filing as New York residents. Which test applies changes your filing strategy, and getting it wrong can mean losing the exclusion entirely.

## How the exclusion works alongside foreign tax credits

The FEIE and the foreign tax credit both reduce your U.S. tax bill on foreign income, but you can't apply both to the same dollar. Exclude a chunk of income under the FEIE, and you lose the ability to also claim a credit for foreign taxes paid on that same income. For some taxpayers, especially those in higher-tax countries, the foreign tax credit alone produces a better outcome than the exclusion.

This is a planning decision, not a compliance trap. The right answer depends on your income level, the tax rate in the country where you're working, and whether you have other U.S.-source income that changes your bracket. Running both scenarios before you file, rather than defaulting to whichever form feels familiar, is where the real savings show up.

## Common mistakes that cost taxpayers this deduction

Most lost deductions come down to timing and documentation, not eligibility. The mistakes we see most often:

- Miscounting qualifying days for the physical presence test, especially around trips home for holidays
- Filing Form 2555 late or not at all, which forfeits the exclusion for that year
- Assuming excluded wages also escape self-employment tax, which they don't
- Overlooking the foreign housing exclusion or deduction that pairs with the FEIE
- Relying on general tax software that wasn't built to flag foreign income elections

Each of these is fixable with a return that's reviewed by someone who knows what to look for before it's filed, not after a notice arrives.

## Frequently Asked Questions About IRS Increases Foreign Earned Income Tax Breaks | Stephen P. Pryor, CPA

**Q: How much is the foreign earned income exclusion now?**
A: The IRS increased the foreign earned income exclusion to $130,000, up from $126,500 the prior year. The amount adjusts annually for inflation, so qualifying taxpayers working abroad should confirm the current figure each filing season rather than relying on last year's number.

**Q: Can I claim both the FEIE and the foreign tax credit?**
A: Not on the same income. You can exclude foreign earnings under the FEIE or claim a credit for foreign taxes paid, but not both on the same dollars. The better option depends on your income level and the tax rate where you're working, which is why this decision benefits from year-round planning.

**Q: Do I qualify for the foreign earned income exclusion if I travel back to the U.S. often?**
A: It depends on which test you use. The physical presence test requires 330 full days abroad in a 12-month period, so frequent U.S. trips can disqualify you. The bona fide residence test allows occasional travel if you maintain genuine, uninterrupted residence abroad for a full tax year.

**Q: What happens if I miss the deadline to claim the FEIE?**
A: Missing the election on Form 2555 can forfeit the exclusion for that tax year, though limited relief exists in specific circumstances. Filing on time with the correct forms, reviewed by someone familiar with foreign income rules, avoids losing a deduction that's often worth tens of thousands of dollars.
