## LinkedIn

A construction company in Port Chester builds a $1.8 million office and equipment yard, then depreciates the whole thing over 39 years, about $51,000 a year. Across town, a real estate investor buys a similar building, runs a cost segregation study, and writes off a third of it in year one. Same tax code, very different outcomes.

Here's what most contractors miss: construction companies are actually better candidates for these studies than real estate investors. Self-performed electrical, plumbing, and sitework already generate the detailed job costing records an engineer needs, which makes the study faster, cheaper, and better documented.

If your company owns the ground it works from, this is worth a real look. Stephen P. Pryor, CPA, Shawn Wilson, CPA, and Denise Romano, CPA walk through the numbers in the full post.
