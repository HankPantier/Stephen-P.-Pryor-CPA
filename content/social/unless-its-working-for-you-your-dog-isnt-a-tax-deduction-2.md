## LinkedIn

A contractor asked us last month if the dog guarding his job site overnight could go on the business return. The answer is sometimes yes, but only with real documentation. Under IRS Publication 535, a guard dog kept at a construction yard or storage lot can qualify as an ordinary and necessary business expense, provided the breed fits the job, the dog lives on-site rather than at home, and the food, vet care, and training costs are billed separately from personal spending. Mix that with a family pet that also naps in the house on weekends, and the deduction falls apart fast. Service and guide dogs work differently: they're a medical expense on Schedule A, subject to the 7.5% AGI floor. Full breakdown, including where breeding dogs fit in, is here: https://pryorcpa.com/resources/unless-its-working-for-you-your-dog-isnt-a-tax-deduction-2
