## LinkedIn

A 2% cash-back card on a $10,000 equipment purchase gives you $200 back, and the IRS treats that as a lower cost basis, not income. That detail matters for construction and real estate clients claiming Section 179 or bonus depreciation, but most business owners never check it because they picked their card for the sign-up bonus instead of their actual spending. A contractor whose costs run through materials and fuel needs a different card than a bar owner spending $40,000 a month with beverage distributors. Sign-up bonuses without a spending requirement are the one place rewards can actually become taxable, which is worth confirming before tax season, not during it. We wrote up how to match card structure to real spending, keep rewards reconciled cleanly in your books, and avoid the tax mismatch that trips up construction, real estate, and hospitality clients most.
