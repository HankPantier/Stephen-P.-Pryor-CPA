## LinkedIn

A $4,000 lumber order that earns $80 in credit card rewards isn't taxable income. It's a rebate, and the IRS treats most business card rewards that way. Sign-up bonuses you get without spending a dime are different: those can be taxed like interest income, and that catches owners off guard at filing season.

The bigger issue is whether your card fits your spending at all. A contractor spending $18,000 a month on materials and fuel gets nothing from a card built around office supply rewards. A bar buying from a liquor distributor five days a week needs rewards on wholesale purchases, not travel points. Shawn Wilson, CPA, and Denise Romano, CPA walk through this exact analysis with new construction, real estate, and hospitality clients during onboarding.
