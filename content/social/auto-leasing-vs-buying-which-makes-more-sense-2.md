## LinkedIn

A contractor comparing a $650-a-month lease to an $850 purchase on a new F-250 is asking the wrong question. The number that actually matters is annual mileage, business-use percentage, and what happens to your tax bill over the next five years.

Here's one piece of the framework: buy a heavy pickup over 6,000 pounds and use it more than 50 percent for business, and Section 179 can let you deduct a large chunk of the price in year one. Lease that same truck and you get a smaller, steadier deduction spread across the term instead. For a contractor racking up 25,000 miles a year, that difference changes the math. For a restaurant owner watching thin margins, the predictable lease deduction might matter more than the upfront write-off.

Stephen Pryor, CPA, walks through the full comparison, including a lease-versus-buy table and the mileage rules that trip people up.
