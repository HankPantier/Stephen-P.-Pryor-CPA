## LinkedIn

A bar in Port Chester was paying $325 a month, $3,900 a year, for five overlapping tools: a POS system, online ordering, reservation software, inventory management, and a scheduling app. Nobody decided to spend that much. It happened $9.99 and $49 at a time, the way subscription creep always does.

Construction firms hit the same wall differently. Estimating software bought for one bid keeps billing $89 a month two years after the project closed, buried among dozens of other charges nobody reviews line by line.

The fix isn't complicated: list every recurring charge with its cost and last-used date, sort by dollar amount, and cancel what's redundant. Stephen Pryor, Shawn Wilson, and Denise Romano, all CPAs, flag exactly these charges during regular bookkeeping for clients on a bundled monthly plan, catching the leak before it becomes a year-long habit.
