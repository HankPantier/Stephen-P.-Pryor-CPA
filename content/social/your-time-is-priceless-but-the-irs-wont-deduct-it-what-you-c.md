## LinkedIn

A contractor donates $150 in lumber to a Habitat for Humanity build in Westchester County. He also spends 20 hours framing interior walls that Saturday. Only one of those is deductible, and it's not the one that took the time.

IRS Publication 526 draws a hard line: the value of your time or professional skill is never deductible, but the cash you actually spend on volunteer work often is. A bar owner who hosts a charity trivia night can write off the food and drinks donated, just not the staff hours worked. A board member driving to two meetings a month can deduct mileage, parking, and tolls too.

The part that trips people up isn't the rule, it's the paperwork. Once your unreimbursed costs for one organization hit $250, you need written acknowledgment before you file, not after.
